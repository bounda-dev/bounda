A command running when `restrict` or `forget` commits no longer stores what it decided. The store checks, in the transaction that writes the events, that nobody they touch is restricted and that every person they were decided on or seal fields of still holds the key read or sealed under: a command running when `restrict` commits is refused with `ProcessingRestrictedError`, a reaction's becomes a dead letter retried once the restriction is lifted, and events sealed under a key `forget` removed meanwhile fail with `ConcurrencyError`, so the command is decided again under the person's fresh key. Events that seal a restricted person's fields are refused too, whoever the command is for. On SQLite and the Durable Object the single writer orders the check; on PostgreSQL the append reads the key and person rows under a share lock, after the append lock that `forget` and `restrict` take first. `AppendArgs` gains `touches`, its `persons` is now checked by the store, `checkAppend` and `ForgottenSinceLoadError` are exported from `@bounda-dev/core/adapter` and `checkSqlAppend` from `@bounda-dev/core/adapter/sql` for adapters to check them with, and `eventStoreContract` takes the whole `Storage` (with `concurrent: false` for one whose calls cannot wait on one another) to pin both races. A dead letter's text is sealed under the key its work read the person's data under, or, when they were forgotten and given a new key since, under a key nobody keeps, so it reads `null` instead of opening under the new key.
