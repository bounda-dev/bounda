Keep the waits of the background personal-data work in the database, with the row they concern: a person the retention could not forget waits on their row in `bounda_persons`, a key the rotation could not rewrap on its row in `bounda_personal_keys`, and a failed forget-ledger reconciliation beside its cursor in `bounda_checkpoints`, each with when it ends and how many attempts failed in a row. Every instance skips what another holds, a restart backs off from where it was, instances failing in the same round wait for one hold instead of adding to it, and `nextDueAt()` names the end of the earliest wait. The waits come from one module, 30 seconds at first and twice as long after each failure up to an hour; a database that cannot keep a wait pauses the job in that instance, growing the same way. The rotation marks itself complete for the configured set of master keys, so `nextDueAt()` never reads the key table while nothing is left to rewrap and the passes read it once a minute across instances; changing the master keys reads it again, and the mark of a configuration no instance runs any more goes after ten minutes. Unwrapped keys are kept five minutes at most from the moment they were unwrapped, dropped by a timer that does not keep a Node process alive (a Durable Object, which such a timer would keep awake, drops them when next read or when evicted), and a person forgotten by the retention or the ledger has their key dropped at once too. `Clock.after` takes `{ background: true }` for such waits. The `PersonStore` and `PersonalKeyStore` contracts gain `hold` and `nextHoldEnd`, a `hold` on each row, and `releasedBy` on their lists; both schemas gain `held_until` and `attempts` columns with an index for held rows.
