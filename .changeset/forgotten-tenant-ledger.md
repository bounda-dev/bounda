A forgotten tenant's forget ledger goes once nothing can need it (articles 5(1)(e) and 17). Once every recipient of the tenant's data is told, the instance that told them clears the tenant's entries and marks from the ledger, the ids of the persons it forgot, restricted, redacted and disclosed to, before it empties the database, and logs `tenant forgotten: its recipients are told, and its forget ledger cleared`; until then it prunes the ledger past `keepFor` as a running tenant's reconciliation does, and a ledger whose reads lag its writes, Workers KV's, is cleared once its `visibilityDelayMs` has passed since the forgetting, which a Durable Object wakes for. A forgotten tenant's app raises no mark in the ledger. Breaking: `ForgetLedger` requires `clear()`, which removes the entries and marks of the ledger's store, leaves other stores' as they were, and resolves `false` when it removed part only, to be called again; a ledger of your own from `defineForgetLedger` implements it, and `forgetLedgerContract` checks it. `createKvForgetLedger` lists every key of its object, then deletes a batch of them a call, within the operations one invocation may make; `read` on it now fills a page past an entry whose value is gone, so a short page means the ledger ends, which the reconciliation and the tenant export page by; the keeper answers `clearLedger(tenant)`, which removes the ledger's alarm with its entries and keeps how long it keeps them.

`TenantForgotten` now tells the recipients of disclosures a restored copy of the database lost: before it is appended, the disclosures the forget ledger holds past what the database applied are recorded again, as a reconciliation would, so a copy restored before the forgetting, or a database not yet reconciled, tells everyone who received data of the tenant. Without a ledger, or with one that cannot be read then, a copy restored before the forgetting presumes every recipient the app declares holds the tenant's data, and tells each `presumed`, logging an error when the ledger failed. A pass of the forgotten app that finds the database restored under it runs the discharge again. `bounda personal-data inventory` says what the forget ledger holds, restrictions, redactions and disclosures included, and that it is kept no longer than until its tenant is forgotten and told.

`app.status().forgottenTenant` (`ForgottenTenantStatus`, `null` while the tenant is not forgotten) says whether every recipient of a forgotten tenant was told, whether the forget ledger holds nothing of it any more (`null` without one), and whether the keys of its encrypted database files are destroyed: the point from which its database may be deleted, which `bounda status` prints as a `forgotten tenant` line. `expectTenantForgotten` now also checks, once every recipient was told, that the forget ledger holds no entry and no mark of the tenant.
