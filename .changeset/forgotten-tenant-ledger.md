A forgotten tenant's forget ledger goes once nothing can need it (articles 5(1)(e) and 17). Once every recipient of the tenant's data is told, the instance that told them clears the tenant's entries and marks from the ledger, the ids of the persons it forgot, restricted, redacted and disclosed to, before it empties the database, and logs `tenant forgotten: its recipients are told, and its forget ledger cleared`; until then it prunes the ledger past `keepFor` as a running tenant's reconciliation does, and a ledger whose reads lag its writes, Workers KV's, is cleared once its `visibilityDelayMs` has passed since the forgetting, which a Durable Object wakes for. A forgotten tenant's app raises no mark in the ledger. Breaking: `ForgetLedger` requires `clear()`, which removes every entry and mark of the ledger's store and leaves other stores' as they were; a ledger of your own from `defineForgetLedger` implements it, and `forgetLedgerContract` checks it. `createKvForgetLedger` lists every key of its object, then deletes each; the keeper answers `clearLedger(tenant)`, which removes the ledger's alarm with its entries and keeps how long it keeps them.

`TenantForgotten` now tells the recipients of disclosures a restored copy of the database lost: before it is appended, the disclosures the forget ledger holds past what the database applied are recorded again, as a reconciliation would, so a copy restored before the forgetting, or a database not yet reconciled, tells everyone who received data of the tenant. Without a ledger, or with one that cannot be read then, a copy restored before the forgetting presumes every recipient the app declares holds the tenant's data, and tells each `presumed`, logging an error when the ledger failed. A pass of the forgotten app that finds the database restored under it runs the discharge again. `bounda personal-data inventory` says what the forget ledger holds, restrictions, redactions and disclosures included, and that it is kept no longer than until its tenant is forgotten and told.
