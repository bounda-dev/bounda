A person's export no longer hands them another person's data, as article 15(4) of the GDPR asks. The error text of a dead letter or a subscriber's last failure is now encrypted under everyone whose data the failed work read, not only the person its event or its process state names: those whose aggregates the commands a policy or process dispatched loaded, and those whose rows a projection read through its `table`. The letter names them in `readPersons`, as does the failure, so `listByPerson`, the export, a restriction, a redaction and the access log reach them, and forgetting any of them makes the text unreadable. A text or a remembered answer encrypted under someone besides the person comes out of their export as `null`, its record naming the field in `withheld` (`ExportedWithheld`: `field`, `reason: "rights-of-others"`, `article: "15(4)"`); `ExportedAnswer.idempotencyKey` and `outcome` are `null` then. `exportPersonalData(personId, { includeWithheld: true })`, and the same `PersonalDataExportOptions` on `streamPersonalData`, the Durable Object's RPC and `connect`, `personalDataExportResponse`'s `init` and `bounda personal-data export --include-withheld`, open those values, still marked, for the controller to weigh; the export's access record gains `withheld` and `includeWithheld`, and names the other persons whose data an opened value may quote. The record of processing lists the subscribers' last failures (`subscriber-failures`). Storage migration 11 adds `read_persons` to the dead letters and the subscriber failures on SQLite, libSQL, the Durable Object and PostgreSQL, indexed for `listByPerson`; the storage contracts cover it.
