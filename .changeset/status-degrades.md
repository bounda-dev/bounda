`app.status()` no longer rejects when a store it reads cannot be read: each part that cannot be read is logged once for the call as `a part of the status could not be read`, with its `part`, and comes back as `{ unreadable: true }` (`UnreadableStatus`), while every other part still comes back. This covers `restoresFound`, `lag`, `subscriberFailures`, `personalDataBackfill`, `forgetLedger`, `tenant`, `keyRotation`, `retention` and `recipients`, as the two purges already did; a database of `schema` that cannot be read is `{ database, unreadable: true }` (`UnreadableSchemaStatus`) beside the others, and a subscriber whose position cannot be read is `{ subscriber, unreadable: true, failing? }` (`UnreadableSubscriberLag`) in `lag.subscribers`, left out of `maxLag`. Narrow on `unreadable` before reading a part; `unreadableParts(status)` names every part that could not be read, empty when the status is whole. `app.status().lag` is now `StatusLag`: `app.getLag()` without `personalDataBackfill`, which is the status' own part. `SubscriberFailureStatus.current` is `null` for an unresolved failure while its subscriber's position could not be read. `bounda status` prints what it read, says `its store could not be read, see the logs` for each other part, names them on stderr, and exits `2`, as it did when nothing could be read, so a check alerts; with `--error-text`, failing to open the failures' text does the same. `bounda recipients` says so on stderr and exits `2` when the recipients cannot be read, and `bounda restored` says the count of restores could not be read back instead of failing.
