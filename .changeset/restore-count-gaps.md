Restores are counted exactly in four more cases. An intention in the forget ledger now keeps where the database stood when it was written, `reached` (`LedgerReached`: the position of the newest event and the restore generation, which the ledger's mark is raised to first), in a new column of the SQL ledgers (their eleventh version) and in the KV entry's metadata: a reconciliation that reads an entry written where the database had gone further than it now stands counts a restore, though the forgetting's commit never reached the ledger, and moves the generation past the entry's, so the entry tells that restore once. A commit the ledger did not take as it returned, or while a call to it hung, is appended again before every look for a restore and at stop, ahead of the stream's copy, and a mark the ledger did not take is raised again on the next look. A restore found while the app runs moves the counter past the ledger's mark, so the next start no longer counts it a second time. The handled restore is one row of `bounda_checkpoints`, so two instances settling at once can no longer leave a mixed pair; the checkpoints' fifth version removes the old `runtime:restore-handled-at` row. `app.restored()` resolves `{ counted }` (`RestoreDeclaration`): `false` when the database stands exactly where handling the last restore left it, which a declaration made again and a copy taken at that moment share; `app.restored({ again: true })` (`RestoredOptions`) and `bounda restored --again` count that copy, and `bounda restored` says when it counted nothing; a declaration that counts nothing starts nothing over. On Workers KV an intention has a key of its own, removed once its committed copy lands, so one written in another location can no longer land over its committed copy whatever KV shows there.
