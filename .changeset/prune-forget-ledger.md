A SQL forget ledger is pruned past `keepFor` with no app of the tenant running. `bounda forget-ledger prune` prunes the tenant `--tenant` names, or with `--all-tenants` every tenant whose entries the ledger's store holds, each through its own configuration, and goes on past a tenant that fails, naming it and exiting with `2`. It removes an entry older than `keepFor` only once the tenant's database no longer needs it: once its reconciliation has read past the entry, or the database holds the forgetting, change of restrictions, redaction or disclosure it records. It stops at the oldest entry the database has yet to apply, as one a copy restored while the app was stopped lacks, and says so, so that copy is still forgotten again at its next start. A forgotten tenant's ledger is pruned past `keepFor` alone, as its app does. It runs safely beside the tenant's running instances, opens the tenant's database only when an entry older than `keepFor` is there, and creates no table in one without them. `pruneTenantForgetLedger({ config, env, tenant, now })` and `listForgetLedgerTenants({ config, env })` from `@bounda-dev/core` do the same in code, and refuse with `ConfigurationError` a configuration without a forget ledger or its `keepFor`, a ledger that expires its entries on its own, and a storage only its host opens. `ForgetLedger` takes an optional `tenants()`, which the SQL and memory ledgers implement; `forgetLedgerContract` checks it.
