Tenant keys and forget ledgers in the app's jurisdiction on Cloudflare (articles 44 to 49). `createBoundaKeeper({ config })` builds a Durable Object class, the keeper, that holds each tenant's key, wrapped under the master key, and its forget ledger in a keeper object of its own, plus one that lists the tenants for a rotation of the master key; `durableObjectTenantKeys({ storage })` and `durableObjectForgetLedger({ storage })` reach it through the `KEEPER` binding (`DEFAULT_KEEPER_BINDING`, or `binding`), in the jurisdiction `storage`'s `cloudflare({ jurisdiction })` names, outside the tenant's object, so a restore of that object carries neither. Its writes compare and set, its reads see every write at once, and with `keepFor` each keeper removes its entries once past it on its own alarm, whether the tenant's object is awake or not. A keeper created in another jurisdiction, or reached for a tenant other than its own, refuses every call with `ConfigurationError`. With a jurisdiction, an app now refuses to start, and `bounda personal-data inventory` to run, on a tenant key store or a forget ledger whose `location` is not that jurisdiction: `kvTenantKeys` and `kvForgetLedger`, since Workers KV holds its pairs to no jurisdiction and caches them outside one, stay for apps that name none. `defineTenantKeyStore` and `defineForgetLedger` take `{ location }` as a second argument, which a store of your own names its place with, and the record of processing states where the forget ledger is kept. Projects from `create-bounda` on Cloudflare export a `Keeper` class, bound as `KEEPER`, and keep their tenant keys and forget ledger there instead of in a KV namespace.
